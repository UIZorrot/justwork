Privacy policy

Your content
belongs to you first.

This policy explains the information JustWork processes to provide encrypted collaborative workspaces, why it is processed, and the choices available to you.

Effective and last updated: August 12, 2026

1. Scope

This Privacy Policy applies to the JustWork Chrome extension, web app, website, and JustWork Backend (together, the “JustWork Services”). When you follow a link to the Chrome Web Store, Stripe, or another third party, that service's own privacy policy applies.

2. Information we process

Workspace data

To provide synchronization and collaboration, the backend processes workspace identifiers, encrypted content payloads, collaborative updates, and the data required for content structure and activity history. Some operational metadata—such as revision numbers, content types, timestamps, member display information, and storage usage—may be stored in a server-readable form to support sync, quotas, history, and conflict control.

Browser-local data

The extension and web app store workspace lists, interface preferences, offline drafts, or pending operations locally. The workspace password is stored in browser local storage only when you explicitly select “Remember password on this device.”

Service operation data

Servers and infrastructure providers such as Cloudflare may process IP addresses, timestamps, User-Agent information, errors, and security logs required to serve requests. We seek to limit this to operation, security, and troubleshooting needs.

Payments and database connections

Stripe processes payment for paid workspaces. JustWork receives the payment status and identifiers needed to fulfill an order and does not require full card details. If you choose custom PostgreSQL, the connection information is submitted after payment and encrypted with a backend routing key.

3. Encryption and workspace passwords

JustWork workspace content is encrypted on the client using a key derived from the workspace password before it is synchronized. The backend does not store the plaintext workspace password and cannot recover it for you.

Encryption does not mean zero metadata.Routing, real-time collaboration, quotas, member presence, and history require the service to process some workspace metadata and network information. End-to-end encryption should not be understood to make all network or business metadata invisible.

Remembering a password locally is a device convenience. Anyone who can access that browser profile or an unlocked session may gain corresponding access.

4. Purposes of processing

  • Create, unlock, synchronize, and display encrypted workspaces;
  • Support real-time collaboration, agent actions, history, and recovery;
  • Apply free and paid workspace quantity, history, and storage rules;
  • Process payment status and optional self-hosted database routing;
  • Secure the service, prevent abuse, and diagnose errors;
  • Meet applicable legal requirements.

5. Third-party services

JustWork may use these categories of providers:

  • Cloudflare: website hosting, content delivery, network security, and related infrastructure;
  • Stripe: checkout and payment status for paid workspaces;
  • Chrome Web Store / Google: extension distribution and updates;
  • User-selected PostgreSQL providers: storage for a workspace only when a paid user actively configures it.

These providers may process necessary information under their own terms. When using your own PostgreSQL database, you are responsible for confirming that its provider and region meet your compliance requirements.

6. Retention and deletion

Data is retained while needed to provide workspaces, history, and security. History events are subject to workspace plan limits, and older events may be removed after the limit is reached. Moving an item to trash is not immediate permanent deletion; permanently deleted content may remain in limited backups or logs until they rotate.

You may delete content or a workspace within the features provided. When a workspace uses your PostgreSQL database, copies, backups, and deletion periods are also governed by your database configuration and provider policy.

7. Your choices

  • Do not enable local password storage;
  • Use the web app, store extension, or manual installation package;
  • Use JustWork-managed storage or optionally configure PostgreSQL for a paid workspace;
  • Review activity history, restore trash, or delete content;
  • Stop using the service and remove local browser site data.

8. Security

We use transport encryption, client-side content encryption, write verification, and infrastructure security measures to reduce risk, but no connected system can guarantee absolute security. Use a strong, unique workspace password, share it through trusted channels, and periodically export or back up important content.

If you discover a suspected vulnerability, do not publicly disclose real workspace credentials or data. Contact the maintainers through the GitHub repository ↗.

9. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as service capabilities or legal requirements change. The new version will be posted here with an updated date; material changes will be highlighted in the product or website where practical.
